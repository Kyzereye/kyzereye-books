## Witness 1 — Dong Zhongshu, *Chunqiu fanlu*

### What this text is

The *Chunqiu fanlu*, or "Luxuriant Dew of the Spring and Autumn Annals," is a collection of essays on cosmology, ethics, and politics written within the Confucian tradition. It is attributed to Dong Zhongshu (179–104 BCE), a leading Han-dynasty Confucian who helped shape imperial ideology. Modern scholars treat the book as a work that was compiled and expanded over time rather than something Dong wrote in one sitting. Chapter 57 is titled *Tonglei xiangdong*, "Things of the Same Category Move Each Other," and it stands as the classical statement of *ganying* (感應), the resonance or stimulus and response that runs between things of the same kind, including between heaven and humanity.

### Brief outline of the passage

The chapter builds its case in five steps.

1. Nature seeks its own kind. Water flows toward damp places and fire turns toward dry ones, because things avoid what differs from them and follow what matches.
2. Where *qi* is the same, things join together. Where musical notes match, they sound together, which the text illustrates with the image of one lute string setting another ringing.
3. Good summons good and bad summons bad. Things of a category respond to one another and rise together, the way a horse answers a horse and an ox answers an ox.
4. Inner and outer share one pattern. A person's yin or yang mood answers the yin or yang rising in heaven and earth, so that worry seeks rest while joy seeks activity.
5. Nothing is truly spontaneous. When a string sounds without being touched, people call it chance, but the text insists that something real is at work.

### Key quotations

> "Good things summon others of their kind; bad things summon others of their kind. Things of a kind respond to one another and arise."

> "Where the *qi* is the same, things join; where the notes match, they resound."

The core line in Chinese reads 美事召美類，惡事召惡類，類之相應而起也。

### Why it fits Chapter 2

This is the most complete ancient statement of the chapter principle outside the Bible. Same kind draws same kind, and that includes inner emotional state calling forth matching outer conditions. Good inner movement summons good, and bad summons bad.

### Context to keep in the prose

*Qi* (氣) is not the same thing as modern talk of vibration. It is the active character or breath-quality of a thing, so the parallel to the Law of Attraction lies in the matching rather than in shared vocabulary. The frame here is cosmic correspondence between ruler, heaven, omens, and human conduct, not a private method for manifesting what a person wants. Present it as a family resemblance to modern teaching rather than the same doctrine.

## Witness 2 — *I Ching*, *Wenyan* commentary on Qian

### What this text is

The *I Ching*, the "Book of Changes," is one of the oldest and most authoritative texts in Chinese civilization, a classic of wisdom, divination, and cosmology built on sixty-four hexagrams. The *Wenyan zhuan*, sometimes called the "Commentary on the Words," is one of the Ten Wings, the body of commentary traditionally attached to the *I Ching* and associated in tradition with Confucius. Scholars generally date this layer to the Warring States through early Han period. The passage used here comments on the Nine in the fifth place of hexagram 1, Qian, the Creative or Heaven. That is the flying dragon line, and the commentary explains why the sage's appearance draws the world toward him.

### Brief outline of the passage

The commentary moves through four beats. It opens with the principle that things of the same sound respond to each other and things of the same *qi* seek each other (同聲相應，同氣相求). It then illustrates the principle from nature, noting that water flows toward what is wet and fire turns toward what is dry, that clouds follow the dragon and wind follows the tiger. It applies this to the sage, saying that when he arises all creatures turn toward him. It closes by generalizing the point: what is born of heaven inclines upward and what is born of earth inclines downward, so each thing follows its own kind.

### Key quotation, Richard Wilhelm translation

> "Things that accord in tone vibrate together. Things that have affinity in their inmost natures seek one another. Water flows to what is wet, fire turns to what is dry… Each follows its kind."

The Chinese reads 同聲相應，同氣相求。水流濕，火就燥。雲從龍，風從虎…則各從其類也。

### Why it fits Chapter 2

The phrase 同氣相求, "same *qi* seek each other," is the closest line in the Chinese classics to the modern claim that energy attracts similar energy. The commentary then applies the idea to character and influence, since the sage's inner quality draws a matching response from the people around him.

### Context to keep in the prose

This is commentary on the *I Ching* rather than the oldest core hexagram text, and it is worth saying so. The idea that things seek one another is about affinity and category, not a guarantee that any particular wish will come true. The passage pairs naturally with Dong Zhongshu, since both come out of the same cultural world of resonance, but it also stands on its own as a short and quotable witness.

- [Chinese text of the *Wenyan zhuan*](http://woniuapp.cn/books/0/wenyanzhuan.html)

## Witness 3 — *Zohar*, arousal from below

### What this text is

The *Zohar*, meaning "Splendor," is the central text of Kabbalah, the Jewish mystical reading of the Torah. It is written in Aramaic and framed as the teaching of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his circle. Tradition ascribes it to the second century CE, while most academic scholars date its publication to thirteenth-century Spain and connect it with Moses de León. The book describes God and creation through the sefirot, the divine emanations, and it teaches that human action, prayer, and inner state below can arouse a matching response above. The technical terms are *it'aruta di-letata*, arousal from below, and *it'aruta di-le'eila*, arousal from above.

### Brief outline of the relevant teaching

Something must first be aroused from below, whether by deed, by speech, or by displaying some likeness to what is being sought, and only then does the arousal come from above. What the lower world shows determines what the upper world gives. Joy below draws joy, while sadness below is met by Judgment, so that a radiant face below is answered by illumination above. The teaching hangs on a scriptural hook, "Serve YHVH in joy" from Psalm 100:2, read to mean that a person's joy draws a supernal joy toward it.

### Key quotations, from Daniel Matt's *Zohar: Pritzker Edition*, vol. 6

> "If She displays a radiant face from below, so She is illumined from above; but if She displays sadness, Judgment confronts Her."

> "A person's joy draws another, supernal joy."

> "In everything there is a requirement to arouse something from below, by action or speech, or to indicate and display some similarity to the act, and then comes the arousal from above."

### Why it fits Chapter 2

The *Zohar* states the chapter principle in emotional terms. A dominant inner state, whether joy or sadness or radiance, draws a matching response in what meets the person. That is inner tone answered by outer and divine response, which is more than simply being good and getting rewarded for it.

### Context to keep in the prose

Say plainly that this text is medieval rather than ancient. Its frame is God, Torah, and the sefirot rather than impersonal energy, so the matching runs through relationship with the divine. The teaching cuts both ways, since sadness below is met by Judgment, and that means it should never be used to blame people for their suffering. The same caution applies here as it does with Numbers 14. On the publishing side, the Pritzker Edition is in copyright, so use brief quotation with attribution.

- [*Zohar*, Chukat ch. 11, arousal from below](https://www.zohar.com/zohar/Chukat/chapters/11)
- [Glossary entry for *it'aruta di-letata*](https://www.torahmitzion.net/kabbalahglossary)

## How the three witnesses work together

Keep them short in the prose, a few sentences each plus one quotation. Their job is to show that the principle is old and turns up across cultures, not to teach Chinese cosmology or Kabbalah in full.

They share a spine. What a person holds inside, whether that is *qi*, an emotional face, inner joy, or dread, belongs to a kind, and that kind draws what matches it.

They also share a limit, and it is worth stating once after all three. These texts do not teach the modern Law of Attraction wholesale. They teach matching inside larger frames, whether the Confucian cosmos, the wisdom of the *I Ching*, or Kabbalah. The Bible adds moral and relational return under God. The parallel is real, but the traditions are not identical.

## Bible 1 — Proverbs 10:24

### What this text is

Proverbs is Israel's wisdom collection, made up of short and compressed observations about how life works under God. Chapter 10 opens the section headed "the proverbs of Solomon," which runs on contrast pairs such as righteous and wicked or wise and fool, each one describing a pattern that links character to consequence. Verse 24 sits inside a cluster about what the wicked and the righteous can expect from the way they live and feel.

### The phrase

The English Standard Version (ESV) reads, "What the wicked dreads will come upon him, but the desire of the righteous will be granted."

The King James Version (KJV) reads, "The fear of the wicked, it shall come upon him: but the desire of the righteous shall be granted."

Two Hebrew words carry the verse. *Pachad* means dread or fear, and it describes what the wicked broods on and expects the worst of. *Taavah* means desire or craving, and it describes what the righteous longs for and orients a life toward. The verbs on either side matter as much as the nouns, because dread is said to overtake a person while desire is said to be granted. In both halves an inner posture meets a fitting outer result.

### Brief outline of the verse in context

The verse sets two inner postures side by side, dread on the part of the wicked and desire on the part of the righteous, then gives each one a matching outcome. What is dreaded comes upon the person, and what is desired is granted. This sits inside a larger contrast block, since the rest of the chapter ties tongue, memory, and conduct to either life or ruin across verses 11 through 32.

### Key quotation

> "What the wicked dreads will come upon him, but the desire of the righteous will be granted."

### Why it fits Chapter 2

This is the clearest single line in the Bible for like meeting like in emotional terms. The dominant inner tone, whether dread or desire, is not sealed off from the rest of life. It belongs to a kind, and that kind draws what matches it. The verse also cuts both ways, which puts it in the same company as Dong Zhongshu and the *Zohar*.

### Relation to the Law of Attraction principle

The Law of Attraction says that dominant thoughts, emotions, and beliefs act like a magnet. Proverbs 10:24 says something close to that in wisdom form, because what a person holds inside emotionally does not stay private. It either overtakes them or is granted to them in what they meet. The resemblance is real: inner state and outer life match. The difference is the setting. Proverbs places the pattern inside wisdom lived under God rather than inside impersonal vibration, and dread and desire here are long-standing orientations of a life rather than single passing thoughts.

### Context to keep in the prose

A proverb observes a pattern rather than issuing a guarantee on a schedule for every case. The words wicked and righteous are moral categories, not older labels for negative and positive thinking. Pair the verse with Matthew 9:29 on faith and Numbers 14:28 on fear-speech, so the reader sees that Scripture names several different inner states that return in kind.

## Bible 2 — Matthew 9:29

### What this text is

Matthew's Gospel presents Jesus as Messiah and teacher. Chapter 9 moves quickly through several scenes: Jesus heals a paralytic in verses 1 through 8, calls Matthew in verses 9 through 13, and answers a question about fasting in verses 14 through 17. Verses 27 through 31 record two blind men who follow him crying out, "Son of David, have mercy on us." Jesus asks whether they believe he is able to heal them, and they say they do. He touches their eyes and speaks the line that matters for this chapter, that it will be done for them according to their faith.

### The phrase

The ESV reads, "According to your faith be it done to you." The KJV reads, "According to your faith be it unto you."

The Greek is *katà tḕn pístin hymôn genēthḗtō hymîn*, which is close to "let it happen to you according to your faith." *Pistis*, translated faith, means trust that Jesus is able to act. It describes an inner agreement with what is being asked rather than general optimism.

### Brief outline of the passage

Two blind men follow Jesus, already convinced that he is the one to ask. Jesus tests that inner posture directly by asking whether they believe he is able. They answer that they do. He then states the matching principle, that it will be done for them according to their faith, and their eyes are opened, so that the outer result answers the inner belief.

### Key quotation

> "According to your faith be it done to you."

### Why it fits Chapter 2

Jesus states the chapter principle almost as a formula, since inner belief sets the outer result. This is the shortest explicit line in the Gospels for the idea that what a person holds in faith receives its match. The structure is the same as the modern claim that a dominant state draws its equivalent, but the faith here is faith in Christ's power rather than confidence in a technique.

### Relation to the Law of Attraction principle

Chapter 1 argues that belief is not decoration but part of the process itself. Matthew 9:29 puts that in Jesus' own mouth: it will be done for you according to your faith. Modern teaching would call this alignment or assumption, while Matthew calls it faith directed toward the one who heals. The shape of the mechanism matches, since inner state leads to a fitting outcome. The frame differs, because Matthew assumes a personal God and Messiah rather than impersonal energy.

### Context to keep in the prose

The healing setting matters, since the faith in view is faith in Jesus' ability rather than faith in an abstract law. Matthew 8:13 says nearly the same thing to the centurion, "as you have believed, so be it done," so use one of the two in the lead trio and cite the other in passing or under the alternatives. This verse carries the positive side of matching, where faith is granted, while Numbers 14:28 carries the negative side, where fear-speech is returned.

## Bible 3 — Numbers 14:28

### What this text is

Numbers tells the story of Israel in the wilderness after the exodus. Chapters 13 and 14 hold the spy narrative. Twelve men scout Canaan, ten of them come back with an evil report, and fear spreads through the camp until the people speak as though defeat were certain and death in Egypt would have been better than going forward. God answers them, and verse 28 is that answer delivered through Moses: as they have spoken, so it will be done to them. The future they receive matches the dominant inner state they had been saying out loud.

### The phrase

The ESV reads, "Say to them, 'As I live, declares the LORD, what you have said in my hearing, I will do to you.'"

The KJV reads, "As truly as I live, saith the LORD, as ye have spoken in mine ears, so will I do to you."

Read the verse together with 14:1–4, which records the people's fear-speech, and 14:26–35, which spells out the sentence of wandering and death in the desert over forty years.

### Brief outline of the passage

The spies see the land, and ten of them fix on giants and danger in 13:31–33. Fear then spreads through the whole camp, showing up as weeping, complaint, and a longing to return to Egypt in 14:1–4. Their speech reveals the inner state plainly, since they say they wish they had died in Egypt and propose choosing a new leader to take them back. God's response in 14:28 turns what they kept saying into what they receive. The outcome matches their words, because the people who said they would die in the wilderness are the ones who do not enter the land they had been promised.

### Key quotation

> "As you have spoken in my hearing, so I will do to you."

### Why it fits Chapter 2

This is Scripture's narrative demonstration of the chapter principle. Dominant dread, rehearsed out loud, draws a matching future. It is the dark mirror of Proverbs 10:24, where dread overtakes a person, and of Matthew 9:29, where faith is granted. Modern language would say the camp's vibration of impossibility drew more impossibility. Numbers shows a people practicing defeat until defeat became the road they walked.

### Relation to the Law of Attraction principle

Chapter 1 makes the point that a person attracts what they are practicing rather than what they casually want. Numbers 14 shows a whole community practicing fear and a return to Egypt, in heart and in speech, and then meeting a future that matches. The parallel is real, since the dominant inner state is not neutral and calls forth its own kind. The Bible names God's agency in that return and treats it as judgment on unbelief rather than the working of an impersonal magnet, but the matching shape is unmistakable.

### Context to keep in the prose

This is story and divine response, not a technique for getting what a person wants. The speech in the passage is the outward expression of inner fear, which ties directly to Chapter 1 on rehearsing lack and dread. Read 13:31–33 and 14:1–4 alongside 14:28, so the reader sees the inner state before the formula that answers it.

## Where the parallel holds and where it breaks

The parallel holds in three places. A dominant inner state, whether thought, feeling, belief, fear, desire, or joy, is not private, and it matches what meets the person. Same kind tends to gather with same kind, which is what Dong Zhongshu and the *I Ching* both say directly. Scripture ties faith, dread, desire, and speech to fitting outcomes again and again.

It breaks over what each tradition thinks is doing the work. Modern Law of Attraction teaching usually frames the mechanism as impersonal energy aimed at desired circumstances. The Chinese texts frame it as *qi* and cosmic resonance inside an order that runs from ruler to heaven to humanity. The *Zohar* frames it through the sefirot and Torah in a medieval mystical setting. The Bible frames it as moral, relational, and communal return under God, where the seed has ethical content and no craving comes with a blank check.
